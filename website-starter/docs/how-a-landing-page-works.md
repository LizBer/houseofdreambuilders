# How a landing page works

Think of it as the conversation you would have if a stranger walked into your practice and you had
two minutes to earn their trust. A landing page is that conversation, in a fixed order, on one page.

## The order, and why
| # | Section | The question in the visitor's head | Real-life equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** | "Am I in the right place?" | The shop window: what you do and who it is for, in one look |
| 2 | **Statement** | "Do they understand me?" | Your first sentence: you name her situation before you talk about yourself |
| 3 | **Hotspots** (the problem) | "That's me." | Pointing at the place that hurts, and saying "this?" |
| 4 | **Offering** | "What exactly do I get?" | Showing the one thing you do, and what it is good for |
| 5 | **Steps** | "What will happen to me?" | Walking her through the door: removing the fear of the unknown |
| 6 | **About** | "Who are you?" | You introduce yourself. People buy from people |
| 7 | **Reviews** | "Do others say it's true?" | A neighbour saying "go there" |
| 8 | **Pricing** | "What does it cost?" | The price card on the counter |
| 9 | **Practical** | "Where, when, how?" | The address, the opening hours, the parking |
| 10 | **FAQ** | "But what if…?" | Answering the doubts before they walk out |
| 11 | **Booking** | "How do I start?" | The appointment book, open on the counter |

Every section does one job and answers one question. If a section doesn't answer a real question
for this visitor, it doesn't belong on this site. Leave it out; don't pad it.

## What gets picked depends on what is known
The kit decides section by section, from what the owner actually has:

- **Has a clear promise and knows the visitor's words** → strong hero, statement and hotspots.
  Without them the page is generic. Get them first.
- **Real reviews** → show them. **None yet** → the section isn't shown on the live site. A page
  with three honest reviews beats one with ten invented ones, and one with none beats a fake.
- **Prices decided** → show them. **Not yet** → the section is left out (or the offering says "on request").
- **Training or credentials** → listed. **Unknown** → the row is left out. Never made up.
- **A place people visit** → address, parking, opening hours, map. **Online or travelling** → e-mail and phone only.
- **A booking tool** → its widget. **None** → the form is the way to start.
- **A portrait of the owner** → a photo in "about". **None** → text only.
- **Sensitive subject (health, children, money)** → privacy link, a careful disclaimer, and no promises.

In a **draft**, everything unknown is visible as a dashed blank so the owner sees what is missing.
On a **live** site, those sections and rows are gone, and the build stops if something is half-done.

## What a good page is not
- Not a menu of ten services. One clear offer is stronger.
- Not about you first. The visitor's situation comes first, then your answer to it.
- Not full of claims you can't back up. Specific and honest beats impressive and vague.
- Not a brochure. It ends on one action: book, or write.
